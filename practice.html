Psychotherapy after traumatic experience

I work with adults living with the effects of traumatic or overwhelming experience. What happened may be recent or long past, clearly remembered or difficult to name, over or still unfolding.

What may bring you to therapy

Sometimes the experience itself is clear. Sometimes what is clearest is what remains: anxiety, low mood, shame, bodily pain or numbness, difficulty trusting, wanting to withdraw, or never quite feeling safe. You do not need to arrive with a complete account of what happened.

Some experiences become traumatic partly because they happened within a relationship, family, community or institution that was meant to provide safety. Therapy may therefore concern not only what happened, but what it did to trust, judgement, belonging and the possibility of depending upon others.

How I work

My approach is Gestalt and trauma-informed. In sessions, we may attend to thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations, ways of withdrawing or protecting yourself, and what happens as difficult experience is approached. There is no requirement to recount everything in detail.

The work is paced carefully, but it is not simply about avoiding what is difficult. We look for a way of approaching experience without either forcing it or allowing fear and withdrawal to determine what remains possible.

Religion and spiritual life

Religious belief, spiritual experience, doubt, conflict, or harm connected with a religious setting may be explored when it matters to you. No belief or disbelief is assumed, and psychotherapy is not spiritual direction or religious formation.
